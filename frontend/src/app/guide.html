How this round works

This is a parent log for the written sit schedule. It follows the data sheet, which is the rule the filled-in example uses. It is not a substitute for the clinician’s plan.

Off-potty interval

  • Start a little shorter than the dry-check day. If he went about every 30 minutes in a pull-up, something like 20 minutes is the idea. The practice sheet starts at 15.
  • A 0-minute row is the first sit after a pull-up comes off. He still wears one for naps, overnight, and leaving the house. That sit stays on the sheet. It does not move the off-potty interval, and it does not count toward the dry streak or the accident streak. The next row keeps the interval that was already in effect.
  • Stay close to the bathroom during the interval.
  • When the timer ends, stop the other activity and go sit, even if he is eating or playing.
  • If you see a precursor, such as touching, ask if he needs to go. Yes means he can go now. No means wait for the scheduled sit.

The sit

  • Say that it is time to go potty. Let him pull his clothes down if he can.
  • He sits for {{ sit() }} minutes, or gets up sooner if he voids in the potty.
  • Sitting should stay pleasant: talk, sing, praise sitting. Block play that fights the sit, such as flushing over and over.
  • {{ reward() }} is only for a pee or poop in the potty. Not for sitting, and not at any other time of day or night. Everyone follows that.

After a toilet void

Praise immediately, then hand over {{ reward() }} immediately. The timing is what teaches him what earned it.

If there is an accident

  • Take him to the bathroom right away and have him sit. Tell him he needs to pee in the potty.
  • He sits the full {{ sit() }} minutes unless he voids in the potty again.
  • If he does void in the potty, praise and reward that the same way as any other success.
  • Record both the accident and the toilet void.
  • Do not reset the off-potty timer. The next scheduled sit stays where it was.
  • Stay neutral while you clean up. No reprimands and no comforting phrases about the accident. Attention goes to the behavior you want to see more of.

When the interval changes

The instruction pages mention adding 5–10 minutes. The data sheet, and the handwritten example, use 5 minutes and an extra condition. This app follows the sheet. The step is {{ step() }} minutes until you change it in Settings.

  • Dry for 3 intervals in a row, and a pee or poop on the toilet in at least one of those three: increase by {{ step() }} minutes.
  • Three dry intervals with no toilet void: the interval stays put.
  • Accidents on 2 intervals in a row: decrease by {{ step() }} minutes.
  • A toilet void on the same row as an accident still counts as an accident. The dry streak starts over.
  • The new interval applies to the next row, which is how the practice sheet is marked.
